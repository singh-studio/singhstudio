The 20-minute brief

What to bring to an intro call, and what to leave at home.

Kris Singh May 2026 · 2 min read

The intro call is twenty minutes on Google Meet. People sometimes apologise for not having “a proper brief” ready. The good news is that a proper brief is usually just the problem, said plainly.

Bring these five things

  1. The problem, in one sentence. Plain words work better than strategy language. “Nobody under 25 registers until the last week” is a good brief.
  2. What “worked” looks like. If we’re celebrating in twelve months, what happened?
  3. Who has to care. The actual person you want to reach.
  4. What already exists. The current site, last year’s campaign, the photo folder nobody opens. If your team already runs its own CMS, bring the login: it tells me more than a description would.
  5. The real deadline. If the date is padded, tell me, because I can work with an honest one.a
a /If you can only bring one thing, bring the problem. We can work out the rest together.

Leave these at home

The slide deck can stay home; it answers questions nobody’s asked yet. Same with the feature list, since features follow from the message. And the apology isn’t needed either: “this might be a silly question” has started some of my best projects.

b /I promise a written scope inside two working days. Hold me to it.
You don’t need the answers on the call. That’s what the twenty minutes is for.

Afterwards, you get a written scope inside two working days: what I’d make, why, and the number, together. You won’t be surprised at proposal stage.b If the scope doesn’t match what you said on the call, I’ll explain it, and you don’t have to accept it.
