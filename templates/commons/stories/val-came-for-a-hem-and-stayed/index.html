Val came for a hem and stayed

Monday 23 November, 2 days ago 1 minute read

Val brought her curtains to the Repair Café in February and asked whether someone could take them up. By May she was hemming other people’s, and these days she has a small waiting list.

The Repair Café runs on the first Saturday of the month, from 10am to 1pm. Last year 214 things came in and 171 left working. Clothes and curtains did best of all, with 58 coming in and 55 going home fixed.

That first Saturday Val sat at the end of a bench with the curtains across her knees while a fixer showed her how to fold the hem and where the pins go. The hem was finished before one o’clock. The next month she was back with a tin of pins, and she offered to look at a pair of trousers that someone had left on the bench.

By the month after that, people were coming to the end of the bench and waiting. Val now brings her own sewing basket and carries it in from the car park herself. She sets up beside the fixers, and the line of people holding curtains, school uniforms and jackets with broken zips is usually the first thing you see when you walk in.

Hemi Walker, who looks after repairs and the building, has given the clothes a bench of its own for the same reason.

Anyone can add their name to the waiting list or bring something along on the day. There is no charge, and koha for parts is welcome. Bring the thing itself, along with anything that goes with it, such as the zip or the cotton you were hoping to match.

The Repair Café still needs fixers for every other kind of thing that comes through the door, and nobody is asked to know much before they start.
