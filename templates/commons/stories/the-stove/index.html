The stove

Friday 30 October, 26 days ago 1 minute read

2012
The year of the first winter in the hall.
11
People at the first Kai Night.
6
Burners on the stove, with two ovens under them.

When the lease on the hall in Tide Street was signed in March 2012, the roof leaked and the stove was very good. The roof has been seen to since, and the stove is still going.

The first winter, in 2012, the people who had taken on the hall spent a lot of Saturdays moving buckets from one drip to the next. The stove was the warmest thing in the building. It has six burners and two ovens, with a pass through to the hall, and it is the stove the kitchen still cooks on.

The first Kai Night had eleven people at it. They sat at one end of a trestle table close to the kitchen, because that was where the heat was, and dinner came through the pass a plate at a time. The roof leaked above the far end of the hall that night as well, so the buckets stayed where they were.

Kai Night now seats ninety at three long tables. Graeme Pollock still switches the ovens on at three o’clock on a Tuesday afternoon. By half past four the onions are on, and by 5.15, when the doors open, the whole hall smells of dinner.

Dinner is served at 5.45pm, with pudding at half past six and the dishes at seven. The doors close at half past seven.

The hall is open 48 weeks a year. The stove rests over the summer break, from 24 December to 20 January, and it is lit again when the house reopens.

Last year it cooked 4,320 plates, which is 48 Tuesdays at 90 a night.

Weeks open each year

48 weeks open, 4 closed. The four empty plates are the summer break.
