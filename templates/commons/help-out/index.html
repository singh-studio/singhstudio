The jobs

Press a job to see what a shift is like, who you would be with and what happens on the first day.

Dish pitTuesdays, 7 to 8.30pm2 needed

What you would do

Scrape, wash, dry and stack the crockery for ninety, while the kitchen wipes down around you.

Who you would be with

Graeme Pollock, who has been cooking since three o’clock, and whoever finished dinner and stayed to dry.

Your first shift

Come at seven and ask for Graeme. He will hand you an apron and show you where everything lives.

I can do that

Pantry packerThursdays, 9 to 10am1 needed

What you would do

Sort the week’s deliveries into the crates, so that by ten o’clock people can walk the table and choose what they need.

Who you would be with

Ana Fifita, who looks after the volunteers and is at the house on Thursdays.

Your first shift

Arrive at nine. The doors open to everyone at ten, so you will see the table fill before anybody walks in.

I can do that

Homework tutorMondays or Wednesdays, 3.15 to 5.15pm3 needed

What you would do

Sit with a kid and work through whatever they are stuck on. You do not have to know the answer, and looking it up together is how most of it goes.

Who you would be with

Priya Nair, who leads Homework Club, and the other tutors. Eleven of you share the room with 32 kids.

Your first shift

Vetting comes first and takes about two weeks. After that, come at 3.15pm in time for snacks at 3.30 and sit beside a tutor for the session.

I can do that

FixerFirst Saturday, 9.45am to 1.15pm2 needed

What you would do

Take whatever has been carried in, a lamp, a zip or a bike, and see what can be done. Last year 214 things came in and 171 left working.

Who you would be with

Hemi Walker, who mends things and minds the building, and the other fixers. Everyone has a bench, and there is a very large box of screws.

Your first shift

Come at 9.45am, a quarter of an hour before the doors open at ten. Hemi will find you a bench.

I can do that

Garden leadEvery other Saturday, 9 to 11am1 needed

What you would do

Hand out the jobs, keep an eye on what needs picking and make sure the tea gets made. It is a morning of beds, buckets and gloves.

Who you would be with

Judith Lowe, who chairs the board and is in the garden every other Saturday, and whoever turns up with boots on.

Your first shift

Be there at nine. The gloves and the tea are supplied and the hat is up to you.

I can do that

Getting vetted

Anybody who will be around kids is checked by the police before their first shift. There are four steps, and the second and third are mostly waiting.

  1. 1

    Fill in the form

    Tell us the job, the days that suit you and how to reach you.

  2. 2

    Bring your ID

    Ana will check it with you at the house.

  3. 3

    Wait about two weeks

    The police vetting runs in the background and costs you nothing.

  4. 4

    Start

    Your first shift is beside someone who has done it before.
