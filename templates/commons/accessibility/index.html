Accessibility

Every page works from the keyboard, and we want to hear if something does not.

The keyboard

Every control can be reached with Tab and pressed with Enter or Space. The first stop on every page is a link that jumps straight to the content. The ring that shows where you are is three pixels thick and held a little way off the thing it circles, dark on light backgrounds and light on dark ones, in every colour set.

The menu opens as a dialog that keeps Tab inside it. Escape closes it and puts you back on the Menu button.

Screen readers

Each page has one main heading, and the headings below it run in order. The header, the content and the footer are marked as regions. Form labels stay on screen, and when a form has a problem the message is tied to its field and focus moves to the first field that needs attention. Nothing is announced as an error before you have sent the form. Confirmations are read out when they appear and take focus.

Colour, type and size

Text has a contrast ratio of at least 4.5 to 1 in all four colour sets and all three type sets in Try it on. We measure the ratios with a tool. Text is set at 15 pixels or larger, and the pages reflow down to a screen 320 pixels wide without sideways scrolling. Zooming the browser makes the text larger and the layout follows.

Movement

Plates are laid on tables as they come into view, panels slide a short way and the picture at the top of the home page moves a little as you scroll. If your device is set to reduce motion, all of that stops and every page shows its final state at once.

Touch and pointer

Buttons are at least 44 pixels tall, and so are the links in the header, the menu and the footer. A link set in a sentence or in a list is only as tall as its text, about 20 to 26 pixels, so it is a smaller target than a button. On Tuesday’s table you can also click a plate to choose that seat. That is a shortcut, and the number box beside it does the same job from the keyboard.

With scripts turned off

Every page can still be read. The parts that need a script, such as the forms and the live week, are replaced by a line saying how to reach us by email or phone.

What does not work well yet

  • The map on the Find us page is a drawing. A screen reader hears one sentence about it, and the same route is written out in the steps beneath it.
  • Calendar files are made in your browser. Some phones ask which app should open them.
  • A very long organisation name in Try it on shrinks the name on the map plate to stay inside it.
  • We have tested these pages ourselves and have not had an outside audit.

The building

The house is step-free from Tide Street. It has an accessible toilet, a hearing loop in the hall, a quiet room, four car parks and two mobility parks at the door. The Find us page describes what happens from the footpath to a seat.

Tell us

If something here does not work for you, email hello@tidestreet.example or call 04 555 0129 and say which page it was. Moana Reweti reads every message, and we will write back with what we have changed or why we cannot.
