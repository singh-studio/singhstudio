About

Tide Street is an old timber hall with a big kitchen out the back. It has been open since March 2012, forty-eight weeks a year, and it runs on a small staff, a board of five and a roster of sixty-four volunteers.

How it started

The hall was old, the kitchen was bigger than the hall seemed to need, and there was one person to take the lease. These are the four things that happened first, in the order they happened.

  1. March 2012

    We took the lease on an old timber hall with a big kitchen out the back, and the house opened that month.

  2. Eleven

    The first Kai Night fed eleven people. Ninety seats came later, and so did the second and third long tables.

  3. The roof

    The roof leaked when the lease began, so mending it came before almost everything else, and the buckets lived in the kitchen for a while.

  4. The stove

    The stove came with the hall and it is still going. Graeme has it lit by three o’clock on a Tuesday.

Sixty-four volunteers

They wash up on Tuesdays, pack the pantry on Thursdays, tutor, fix and garden. If you would like to join them, there is a form on the Help out page.

Where the money goes

Last year the house cost $148,600 to run. Each plate below is about $1,486, so a hundred of them make the year.

Judith reads these numbers out at the annual meeting on the third Thursday of March, and everybody can ask about them. If you would like to put plates on the table, giving is on the Give page.

How it’s run

Four things decide how the house is run.

  • The board

    Judith Lowe chairs a board of five. The board sets the budget and signs off the accounts, and Judith presents them to anyone who comes to the annual meeting.

  • Police vetting

    Anyone working with children is police vetted first. It takes about two weeks and costs you nothing, and Ana Fifita looks after it.

  • If something goes wrong

    Email hello@tidestreet.example and say it is a complaint. Moana Reweti reads every one and will tell you what happens next. If the complaint is about Moana, say so in the subject and Judith Lowe will answer it instead.

  • What we keep about you

    Only what you give us on a form, and only to run the house. We do not share it. The privacy page lists it all.
