Start with the message

Why the website, film or campaign comes after the message.

Kris Singh June 2026 · 3 min read

Most briefs arrive pre-solved. “We need a new website.” “We need a video.” Someone chose the artefact before anyone said the problem out loud, usually because it’s what the last supplier sold.

Here’s the method I use instead. Three questions, in order.

What has to be true afterwards?

What must be different once this work has done its job: more registrations, a room that understands the change, or a funder who says yes. If I can’t describe that, nothing I make can achieve it.

a /People act one at a time, so I write for one person.

Who has to act?

Messages reach one person at a time, someone with a Tuesday to get through, like a parent deciding whether camp is safe, a board member skimming on a phone between meetings. Name that person and half the design decisions make themselves.a

Where will they meet it?

Only now does the artefact conversation start, and it depends on which route works. A website carries registration well and urgency badly. A film builds feeling but can’t answer an FAQ at 11pm. A campaign’s job is to move people to wherever the actual decision gets made. For Amplify, that has meant three years of rebuilding the conference site itself, not just running ads about it.

I like to know the destination before I pick how to get there.
I’ve built the wrong artefact for the right brief before. It’s an expensive way to learn that.
b /This is also why I’ll sometimes talk you out of the thing you came to buy. It costs less than building the wrong thing.

That is why working across web, film and audio helps: I’m not tied to selling one thing. A web shop will suggest a website, a video house will suggest a film, and I’d rather tell you when you need neither.b

Bring me the problem before you’ve solved it. The three questions work just as well on a project that’s already underway, so if you’re mid-brief and something feels off, that’s usually why.
