Tino/Taonga

A Squarespace hub for Next Gen leaders, with a resource library, a team finder and pages the team edits as posts.

At a glance

What they needed

The Next Gen team supports everyone leading children’s, youth and intergenerational ministry in The Salvation Army across Aotearoa, Fiji, Tonga and Samoa. Leaders usually arrive wanting one thing: a resource for this week, the right person to talk to, or the details of a camp.

What I did

The team runs the site themselves, so I built it on Squarespace and wrote custom code for what Squarespace can’t do out of the box. The content lives in four blogs, two of them hidden from visitors and working like databases, and scripts read those posts to build what visitors see. The team only ever edits posts.

What changed

The site is live, and the team runs it themselves.

The front door

Tino Taonga takes its name from the idea at the centre of the team’s work: that children and young people are invaluable treasures. The homepage opens with a greeting in the territory’s languages and puts the three most common jobs up front: search the resources, jump to an area, or find your Next Gen person.

The film plays inside the speech bubble from the brand, and scrolling opens it out into the Tino Taonga statement. Before any of that, a line of scripture comes up, chosen for the season of the church year.

Everything below the opening fills itself in: the next events and whether they’re open, programmes grouped by area, the team, the newest resources and the latest newsletter.

In Squarespace
One post holds the headline, greeting, film and statement. Every other section reads from the four blogs.
In code
A page-header script builds the page. The scripture intro sits in the site-wide header.

The resource library

Guides, forms, Bible studies and session plans, filtered by area and programme, with a live count beside every option.

Each card says what it is before you open it (PDF, form, guide or how-to) and roughly how long it takes to read. The filters write to the address bar, so a leader can send a colleague the exact list they’re looking at.

In Squarespace
An ordinary blog. Categories are the areas and tags are the programmes.
In code
A licensed filter plugin, set up and restyled, plus a script that reads each post to label its card.
nextgen.salvationarmy.org.nz/resources

The team finder

Sixteen people across eight regions, from Northland to Samoa. A leader can search for a corps, a town, a name or a need (“kids camp” works, and so do “Palmy” and “the Mount”), pick a region on the map, or finish the sentence “I’m in anywhere and I need help with anything”.

The grid narrows as they go, and choosing someone opens their profile above it. Once the search scrolls off screen, a Find button sits next to Menu so it’s always to hand.

In Squarespace
A hidden blog, one post per person. Categories say what they help with, tags say where they work.
In code
One code block draws the map, search, grid and profiles from that blog’s feed.
nextgen.salvationarmy.org.nz/team

Programme and event pages

Seventeen pages, from Kids Camp to Mainly Music, share one layout. The team writes each page as a post.

Lines like When:, Where: and Cost: become the At a glance panel, a Status: line becomes the pill under the title (its dot turns red once a camp closes or fills), and the photo’s crop follows the focal point they set on the post’s image.

Headings, lists and quotes from the editor’s own toolbar are styled to match the site, so nothing relies on code the team can’t see. If a post is missing or half filled in, the custom part steps aside and the page falls back to standard Squarespace.

In Squarespace
The other hidden blog, with one post for each page. The post’s address matches the page it fills.
In code
A footer script and stylesheet build the panel, the status pill and a strip of related resources.

Resource posts and profiles

On a resource post, the file becomes a proper download card named after the resource, and a Talk to someone box suggests the staff who help with that area.

Each person’s page is built from the same record as the finder, and lists the resources and news that match what they help with. Nobody keeps these links up to date by hand. They come from the categories the team already uses.

In Squarespace
The same categories across the News, Resource Hub and Team Members blogs.
In code
Footer scripts that match posts and people by category and add the cards.

More details

The handover

I handed the site over with a 37-page guide for the team, written for people who don’t think of themselves as technical and illustrated with screenshots from their own editor.

It walks through every part they edit (programme pages, the team, News, the Resource Hub and the homepage) and ends with checklists and a one-page quick reference.
