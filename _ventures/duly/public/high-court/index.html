How the High Court Rules counts a working day

Under the High Court Rules, a day is not a working day if it is:

  • a Saturday or Sunday
  • Waitangi Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, Anzac Day, the King's Birthday, Matariki and Labour Day
  • the weekday Waitangi Day and Anzac Day are observed on when they fall on a weekend
  • any day from 25 December to 15 January

Worked example

Under the High Court Rules (High Court Rules 2016, r 1.3(1)), 25 working days after Tuesday 1 December 2026 is Wednesday 27 January 2027. Skipped besides weekends: Fri 25 Dec 2026 – Fri 15 Jan 2027 (25 December – 15 January period).

When a notice counts as given

  • Served electronically after 5pm or on a non-working day (next working day) (r 6.6(3))
  • Document exchange (2nd working day) (r 6.6)
  • Posted to a PO box (5th working day) (r 6.6)

Registries also close on court holidays: Good Friday to Easter Tuesday, 24 December to 3 January, and the anniversary day where the court is (r 3.2). If time runs out on a day the registry is closed, filing on the next day it is open is in time (r 1.18). Statutory appeal periods use the Senior Courts Act definition, which also skips that anniversary day.

Questions

Does the day I received it count?

Not normally. Where a period runs “after” a day, that day is left out, so day 1 is the next working day. Duly counts that way by default. If your contract counts “from and including” a day, open More options and tick “Count the start date itself as day 1”.

Can I rely on this?

Duly applies High Court Rules 2016, r 1.3(1) and shows every day it skipped, so you can check the answer by hand. It isn’t legal advice: the timeframe provision itself, any extension, a contract term or a court direction can change the answer.
