How Duly checks itself

A deadline tool is only useful if you can see why it gave the answer it did. So Duly shows its working, cites its sources and tests itself against the official record.

Holidays

Public holidays come from the Holidays Act 2003 (s 44, and the Mondayising rules in ss 45 and 45A), Matariki from Schedule 1 of Te Kāhui o Matariki Public Holiday Act 2022, and Easter from the Gregorian calculation. Provincial anniversary days follow the rules MBIE publishes. They are custom rather than statute, and the engine says so where observance varies (Otago, Westland).

The engine is tested against every public-holiday table on employment.govt.nz from 2010 to 2027: all 187 national holiday observations and 216 anniversary days match. That testing found two errors in the published tables (the weekday given for Anzac Day 2027, and South Canterbury’s 2022 anniversary day, which moved because of the Queen Elizabeth II Memorial Day). Matariki dates to 2052 are checked against Te Papa’s published schedule. Duly won’t answer for dates after 2052, because Matariki has not been set beyond then.

How counting works

  • A period of working days “after” a day leaves that day out: day 1 is the next working day.
  • Counting backwards finds the N-th working day before a date, not counting the date itself.
  • Calendar-day periods can be moved to the next working day where a rule says so, as IRD due dates are.
  • Every answer comes with each skipped day and its reason, the citation, the engine version and the date the rules were last checked.

What Duly doesn’t do

It doesn’t know your facts. Extensions, transfers, the hour of day something arrived, a contract that defines working day differently or a court direction can all change a deadline. Duly isn’t legal advice, and the terms say so plainly.
