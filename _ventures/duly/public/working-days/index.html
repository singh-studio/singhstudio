How the Legislation Act counts a working day

“Working day” means a day of a week other than— (a) a Saturday, a Sunday, Waitangi Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, Anzac Day, the Sovereign's birthday, Te Rā Aro ki a Matariki/Matariki Observance Day, and Labour Day; and (b) a day in the period commencing with 25 December in a year and ending with 2 January in the following year; and (c) if 1 January falls on a Friday, the following Monday; and (d) if 1 January falls on a Saturday or a Sunday, the following Monday and Tuesday; and (e) if Waitangi Day or Anzac Day falls on a Saturday or a Sunday, the following Monday.Legislation Act 2019, s 13

Under the Legislation Act, a day is not a working day if it is:

  • a Saturday or Sunday
  • Waitangi Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, Anzac Day, the King's Birthday, Matariki and Labour Day
  • the weekday Waitangi Day, Anzac Day, New Year's Day and 2 January are observed on when they fall on a weekend
  • any day from 25 December to 2 January

Questions

Does the start day count?

It depends on the wording (Legislation Act 2019, s 54). A period “after” or “from” a day, or “within” a number of days of it, leaves that day out. A period “starting on” or “beginning with” a day includes it: tick “Count the start date itself as day 1”.

What if the last day isn’t a working day?

If something must be done on a day, or by the last day of a period, and that day isn’t a working day, it can be done on the next working day (s 55).

Does the day I received it count?

Not normally. Where a period runs “after” a day, that day is left out, so day 1 is the next working day. Duly counts that way by default. If your contract counts “from and including” a day, open More options and tick “Count the start date itself as day 1”.

Can I rely on this?

Duly applies Legislation Act 2019, s 13 and shows every day it skipped, so you can check the answer by hand. It isn’t legal advice: the timeframe provision itself, any extension, a contract term or a court direction can change the answer.
