How IRD due dates counts a working day

Under IRD due dates, a day is not a working day if it is:

  • a Saturday or Sunday
  • New Year's Day, 2 January, Waitangi Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, Anzac Day, the King's Birthday, Matariki, Labour Day, Christmas Day and Boxing Day
  • the weekday New Year's Day, 2 January, Waitangi Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, Anzac Day, the King's Birthday, Matariki, Labour Day, Christmas Day and Boxing Day are observed on when they fall on a weekend

Anniversary days don’t move electronic payments: if a due date falls on your anniversary day, pay electronically by the due date itself (IRD Standard Practice Statement 21/03).

IRD’s due dates (such as 15 January) are not moved by the 25 December to 15 January period in the tax Acts’ own definition of working day.

Questions

What about the 25 December to 15 January period?

The Income Tax Act and GST Act define working days to exclude 25 December to 15 January, but IRD still sets due dates such as 15 January, and the weekend and public holiday rule is what moves them.

Does the day I received it count?

Not normally. Where a period runs “after” a day, that day is left out, so day 1 is the next working day. Duly counts that way by default. If your contract counts “from and including” a day, open More options and tick “Count the start date itself as day 1”.

Can I rely on this?

Duly applies Inland Revenue, “When to pay”; Legislation Act 2019, s 55 and shows every day it skipped, so you can check the answer by hand. It isn’t legal advice: the timeframe provision itself, any extension, a contract term or a court direction can change the answer.
